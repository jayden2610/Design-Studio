# Monochrome Branding: Identity Portfolio Workflow

## Purpose

Create a coherent series of speculative F&B brand identities that all feel authored by Monochrome Branding, while making each business unmistakably its own.

Every project uses the same five-deliverable structure. The identity changes through its food ritual, brand world, visual language, and practical applications—not by changing the portfolio format every time.

## Required First Step for Any Full Build

Before beginning any full Monochrome Branding identity build, read this entire document and use it as the project's operating brief. Do this before ideation, image generation, mockup work, portfolio layouts, or Oil Motion production.

If an existing project brief conflicts with this document, preserve the approved project-specific decisions but flag the conflict before producing new assets.

## The Studio Signature: What Stays Consistent

Every concept should retain these qualities:

- One clear, memorable food or service ritual.
- An illustrated primary mark built around a hero object, a single action, and one business cue.
- Handmade ink character: a confident silhouette, imperfect contour, and selective interior detail.
- Generous breathing room and an editorial case-study layout.
- A warm, local, human point of view rather than a generic restaurant identity.
- Believable real-world proof: applications that make the fictional business feel operational.
- A short motion study derived directly from the primary illustration.

The target is a recognisable studio system, not five versions of the same brand.

## The Five Deliverables

### 01 — Opening / Brand in the World

**Job:** Make someone want to see the project before they understand it.

- Editorial project headline and one-sentence positioning.
- A hero real-life mockup, usually exterior signage, shopfront, market stall, or a highly distinctive interior moment.
- Brand name and visual system shown at realistic scale.

**Success check:** Without reading the explanation, the viewer can identify the type of business and the feeling it offers.

### 02 — Identity Rationale / Primary Mark

**Job:** Explain the core idea and make the identity assets easy to inspect.

- Brand name, short positioning statement, and personality copy.
- Primary illustrated logo or mark on a calm field.
- Palette, type direction, or a small selection of graphic tokens when useful.
- The one-sentence ritual behind the illustration.

**Success check:** The mark reads in three seconds and the explanation matches what is visible.

### 03 — Practical Brand World

**Job:** Prove the identity works beyond a logo.

- One major branded environment or operating asset: storefront, counter, menu, packaging, cart, or window.
- One secondary application set: stickers, stamps, labels, takeaway pieces, food variants, or loyalty materials.
- Copy and visual treatment that reinforce the brand's particular customer experience.

**Success check:** The applications are appropriate to how that specific business actually serves customers.

### 04 — Core Illustration / Reduction Test

**Job:** Show the idea at its clearest.

- Full primary illustration on white, warm paper, or the brand's quietest supporting field.
- Optional small-mark or one-colour reverse version beside it when it adds useful proof.
- No presentation clutter that competes with the silhouette.

**Success check:** The hero, action, and business cue remain understandable with labels and hatching removed.

### 05 — Oil Motion Study

**Job:** Make the illustration feel alive while proving it can become a living brand asset.

- Four keyframes by default; use five or six only when the ritual has genuinely distinct beats.
- The animation uses the same hero object, action, composition, and visual world as Deliverable 04.
- A square or vertical social-ready render, plus the local scroll-controlled `demo.html` source.

**Success check:** The motion tells the same single story as the primary illustration. It is not a second logo idea.

## What Can Change Between Brands

Change enough of the following variables that each concept is distinct, while retaining the studio signature above.

| Variable | Questions to decide | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category and offer | What is being sold and when is it consumed? | morning bagels, lunch gimbap, bakery conchas, kopi and kaya toast, late-night noodles |
| Core customer feeling | What should the customer feel before ordering? | slow and welcoming, brisk and precise, soft and celebratory, everyday and familiar, energetic and nocturnal |
| Food or service ritual | What is the one verb in the illustration? | turn, roll, cool, pour, lift, tear, stamp |
| Hero object | What must read first? | hanging bagel sign, gimbap roll, concha, kopi cup, noodle bowl |
| Business cue | What single object proves the business context? | awning, bamboo mat, cooling rack, kopitiam table, illuminated stall sign |
| Composition and crop | How close, wide, vertical, or horizontal is the story? | opening ritual, close handoff, product action, counter vignette |
| Line language | How does the ink behave? | fine and airy, graphic and dense, rounded and playful, loose and fast, bold and high-contrast |
| Palette and contrast | What is the light/dark balance and accent colour logic? | quiet cream/cocoa, bright orange/burgundy, seaweed green/black, kopi brown/yellow, black/chilli red |
| Typography | How should the voice sound in words? | elegant editorial serif, rounded neighbourhood display, condensed utility type, hand-painted stall lettering |
| Supporting graphic device | What repeatable secondary element belongs only to this brand? | scallop pattern, rice-grain dots, cup rings, steam lines, receipt strips, tiled grid |
| Copy voice | What does the brand say and how? | poetic and slow, warm and direct, playful, practical, late-night bold |
| Real-world applications | What does this business genuinely need? | bakery box and window decal, gimbap sleeve and menu board, kopi cup and laminated menu, noodle bowl and illuminated fascia |
| Motion arc | What moves across the keyframes? | hand opens sign, mat rolls, bun lands on rack, kopi pours, noodles lift |

### Minimum differentiation rule

For every new identity, deliberately change at least five variables, including:

1. The category or offer.
2. The ritual and hero object.
3. The business cue or composition.
4. The palette and contrast relationship.
5. The real-world application set.

Do not reuse the exact hand pose, awning, shopfront template, palette, or sticker arrangement from another case study merely because it worked before.

## End-to-End Workflow

### Phase 1 — Choose the brand world

1. Choose a food category that strengthens the portfolio's range.
2. Define the business in one sentence: customer, offer, time of day, and feeling.
3. Choose one customer promise or headline.
4. List the business's real operating touchpoints before making visuals: storefront, menu, packaging, receipt, product label, tableware, delivery, or social.

**Output:** a one-page brand brief.

### Phase 2 — Write and test the visual story

1. Write the illustration sentence in this form: `A [hand/person/tool] [one verb] a [hero object] at/with a [single business cue].`
2. Identify the hero, action, and context cue.
3. Choose one composition system: close service gesture, product action, opening ritual, or counter vignette.
4. Create two to three variations by changing only crop, angle, or pose.
5. Select the fastest three-second read.

**Gate:** If the concept needs two verbs, multiple heroes, or a full shop scene to explain itself, simplify before continuing.

### Phase 3 — Build the identity foundation

1. Render or draw the primary illustration in black ink first.
2. Remove nonessential texture before adding any detail.
3. Test the silhouette at small size and create a reduced small mark if needed.
4. Select the brand's type direction, palette, contrast ratio, copy voice, and one supporting graphic device.
5. Establish the final brand name, tagline, and terminology.

**Gate:** Use one brand name consistently across all five deliverables, mockups, image-generation prompts, asset filenames, and motion frames.

### Phase 4 — Create real-world proof

1. Pick one hero environment for Deliverable 01.
2. Pick the most commercially believable primary application for Deliverable 03.
3. Pick a secondary set that makes sense for the category—not a generic repeated sticker board.
4. Generate or compose realistic mockups with the actual identity assets applied.
5. Check that scale, material, lighting, and customer use feel plausible.

**Guideline:** Use image generation to create the environment and context. Preserve the approved identity artwork when placing it in the environment; do not rely on generated text to reproduce the final logo or wordmark accurately.

### Phase 5 — Compose the portfolio set

1. Design the five deliverables in the fixed order above.
2. Keep the presentation grid, spacing discipline, and editorial restraint consistent with the wider portfolio.
3. Let the new brand's palette, imagery, type, and copy carry its individuality.
4. Review the complete sequence as an Instagram carousel, then as standalone posts.

**Gate:** A viewer should recognise all five slides as one brand before seeing the next case study.

### Phase 6 — Build Oil Motion

1. Inventory the approved primary illustration and choose four semantic motion states: opening/approach, contact, main action, resolved pose.
2. Write a shared style lock: illustration style, camera, crop, palette, line weight, subject invariants, and motion direction.
3. Generate or draw only the needed sequential frames; keep the subject, proportions, and composition fixed.
4. Remove chroma/background, save the transparent frames as `final/keyframes-alpha/K0.png` onward, and build `demo.html`.
5. Verify the frame order, title, reduced-motion behaviour, and final social render.

**Gate:** The motion cannot introduce a different brand name, different logo, or unrelated action.

### Phase 7 — Final quality review

Run this checklist before publishing:

- Is the business name identical everywhere?
- Does the primary mark read in three seconds?
- Is there one hero, one action, and one context cue?
- Does each application make sense for this exact business?
- Is the palette distinct from the other concepts while still restrained?
- Is the copy voice specific to this brand?
- Does the core illustration match its mockups and motion frames exactly?
- Do the five deliverables work as both a sequence and standalone social posts?
- Have at least five differentiation variables changed from the previous identity?

## Suggested Initial Portfolio Range

1. **Bagel opening ritual:** slow neighbourhood morning; awning and hanging sign.
2. **Gimbap roll:** precise lunch ritual; bamboo mat and takeaway sleeve.
3. **Concha cooling:** soft bakery ritual; cooling rack and pastry box.
4. **Kopi and kaya toast:** familiar daily ritual; kopitiam table and laminated menu.
5. **Noodle lift:** high-contrast evening ritual; noodle bowl and illuminated stall sign.

Start by completing three fully developed projects. Add the fourth and fifth only after the first three prove that the system remains distinct in practice.
